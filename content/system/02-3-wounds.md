# Wounds

Characters have a number of wounds on their character sheet, which are gradually filled by injuries.

These are the wounds and their effects:

| Wound level | Gameplay effects | Description / examples |
|---|---|---|
| **lvl 1** | Flavor effects or status effects | Cuts, bruises, small wounds. Also status effects: exhaustion, being drunk, being confused etc. |
| **lvl 2** | −1d8 on all affected checks | Sprains, burns, larger stab or cut wounds. Stronger status effects. |
| **lvl 3** | −1 success on all affected checks | Broken bones, large wounds, heavy blood loss |
| **lvl 4** | The character suffers permanent damage or dies | Lost limbs, impalement, complete burns |

A character's wounds can be written like this:

**X - Y - Z**

X is the number of lvl 1 wounds, Y the number of lvl 2 wounds and Z the number of lvl 3 wounds. This notation is only relevant in certain chapters of the rules – on the character sheet, wounds should be shown as a table, since every filled wound describes which injury it is.

> [!example] A character with 5 - 3 - 1 wounds after a fight
> **lvl 1:** cut on the leg · blow to the head · exhaustion · ☐ · ☐  
> **lvl 2:** sprained arm (right) · ☐ · ☐  
> **lvl 3:** broken hand (left)

Wounds are assigned by level: when a character takes damage, the GM tells the player what kind of wound has to be filled.

If all wounds of a level are filled, the next higher level is checked for a free wound.

If all wounds upwards are filled when a character takes a wound, the character fills a lvl 4 wound – which means drastic consequences or even death.

Wounds can be healed, either by magic (Life element) or by care and rest.

The latter means that characters can give each other first aid or be treated by doctors.

With the next rest, a lvl 1 or a lvl 2 wound is then reduced by 1.
