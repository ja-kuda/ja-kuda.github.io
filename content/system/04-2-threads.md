# Threads

**Null** is what exists behind the fabric of reality – an indescribable plane of cosmic energy, a state in which time, space and physical laws do not exist, a space in which everything and nothing exist at the same time and there are no recognizable patterns.

The existence in which Ambition takes place is shielded from this Null by the fabric of reality, which allows structures to form and life to flourish.

Normally the fabric is stable and impenetrable to the Null. But when arcane mages begin to manipulate this fabric and restructure it for their purposes, it loses structure and hold. Single threads come loose – exactly those threads that the arcane weavers bring into new forms.

And as these threads are pulled out, the border between reality and Null blurs.

The more threads are pulled, the more arcane magic is worked in one place, the thinner the fabric becomes – and in the worst case it can tear, often with devastating consequences.

---

## Threads in play

For characters who want to use arcane magic, this means they always walk a tightrope and have to weigh how often and how strongly they want to use their magic – because every spell needs threads.

> [!rule] Thread pool
> Characters have a default pool of **9 threads** (plus half their Lore skill, rounded down) that they can use within a period of time before they overload.

The number of dice in a spell check equals **1 + the number of threads pulled + any modifiers**.

Conflict from freeform magic mainly shows as so-called **feedback**, which strikes back at the mage and endangers them, as well as through influence on the surroundings or side effects of the spell itself.

## Feedback of a spell & arcane scars

Conflict from casting freeform magic shows not only in the effects of the spell itself (e.g. an unexpected side effect, a larger or smaller area, additional effects and so on), but also in consequences that affect the mage and reality itself.

The more threads are pulled for a spell, the greater the impact on the weaver and on the fabric of reality.

This can show as temporary or permanent effects – up to arcane scars:

> [!rule] Feedback by threads pulled
> | Threads pulled | Feedback |
> |---|---|
> | **0–3** | None |
> | **4–6** | + Consequences |
> | **7–8** | ++ Consequences |
> | **9+** | +++ Consequences **and an arcane scar** |

Arcane scars mark the character as an arcane weaver and always depend on the arcane element used.

> [!example]
> A lightning mage might receive a scar that twitches with electricity, a time mage a scar that looks aged, or an essence mage a scar that looks scaly or like bark.

The scars should have small passive effects or be noted as traits on the character sheet.
