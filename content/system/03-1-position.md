# Position and scale

Every action of a character that requires a check is based on a starting situation – the **position**. It describes what is happening in the story, but also influences the gameplay and the chances of success of an action.

The **scale** is a quick overview of how position, the check's result and the resulting conflict and effect interact.

| Below target | Target | Above target |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| **+Conflict · −Effect** | **Conflict · Effect** | **−Conflict · +Effect** |

The target on the scale is the number of successes needed to land in the yellow area of the scale, the middle. Depending on the target, the number of successes needed for the red and green areas follows.

## Choosing the target

As a rule of thumb, the following values can be used based on the starting situation of a check:

| Position | Target | When to use |
|---|:-:|---|
| **Safe** | 0 | The red area is dropped. For these checks, ask whether rolling is necessary at all, or whether the situation can be resolved through narration. |
| **Unsafe** | 1 | The default for a normal situation, since checks are mainly there to resolve situations with an uncertain outcome. |
| **Risky** | 2 | Situations in which the characters take a relevant risk but can still realistically reach their goal. |
| **Desperate** | 3 | Situations in which the characters have to go beyond their abilities to avoid severe consequences. |
| **Absurd** | 4+ | Situations that seem almost impossible and carry a near-guaranteed risk of severe consequences. |

Setting the target is generally up to the GM, who names a number based on the character's situation.

However, players should have the chance to influence this value, for example by:

- Shifting their position – into a riskier situation for a higher potential effect, or into a safer one at the cost of effect
- Naming character traits that could change the situation
- Helping other characters or getting help from them
