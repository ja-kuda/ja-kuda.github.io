# Legends and songs

Stories told in taverns, temples and nurseries across Akudia. Some are true, some are half true, and some are whatever the teller needs them to be.

> [!note]
> These are told *in-world*. What the common folk believe isn't always what really happened – the hidden truths are in the boxes.

---

## The Lord of Tears

The Lord of Tears is called the father of necromancy. He is said to have been the first to discover this kind of magic and to use it.

Over countless years of training, experimenting and refining his gift, he taught others who felt the same bond to this unknown magic. It wasn't long before people began to revere him. Others used necromancy to do harm and to attack their neighbours.

Every use of necromancy fed the Lord of Tears a share of the victims' souls and life force, granting him an unnaturally long life.

When he saw the damage some did with his art, he began to teach what he knew about life and death as well as he could, so that it would be used for good. Over the following centuries he sought out those with the gift – which grew stronger and more common – and offered to teach them. He only helped those who would use it well: he taught farmers to command the remains of their animals for simple labour, or how to speak with the dead to say a last farewell to a loved one.

After some decades of his pilgrimage, lords and even the crown began to feel threatened by the new abilities of the common folk. They started to hunt down and kill children who showed a talent for necromancy.

**One of those children was Rogamon.**

The Lord of Tears still believed in the good of necromancy, and when Rogamon escaped captivity he took him as his student. Rogamon soon showed great talent – and a hunger for revenge and destruction. Master and student parted ways when Rogamon was a young man, and met again only after Rogamon had used his gift to conquer Winterglehn and parts of the Expansion.

The Lord of Tears ordered him to stop. Rogamon refused, and the two fought. In the end the Lord of Tears had to admit that he was no match for Rogamon's raw power, and he vanished.

Only after Rogamon's reign was he given the name *Lord of Tears*: a figure once known as a folk legend had become the tragic cause of the greatest catastrophe the continent had ever seen.

> [!secret] Hidden truth
> The Lord of Tears still lives, hidden in **Driftholz**. He blames himself for Rogamon's age of darkness, but he cannot end his own life – he is still kept alive by all the souls ever harmed by necromancy.

## The Raven who cleanses the world

After the end of the Conclave, the remnants of the **Cabal of Korvus** turned to a new master: the Raven who will cleanse the world.

This mystic being is said to herald the end of civilisation as it is known and to free the world of the carrion of mankind. Nobody knows who or what the Raven is, and its followers are forever searching for clues and signs. See [Factions](#factions).

---

## The Leviathan

Far in the north, beyond the last spits of land of the Ice Waste, a gigantic being is said to sleep in the eternally frozen sea. Should it ever wake, it would bring death and destruction over the continent.

---

## The Stone Wanderer

A legendary warrior of Atania who died in a volcanic eruption.

In the centuries that followed, stories and rumours kept appearing of a warrior made of stone, covered in glowing magma, wandering the land.

## Songs and poems

> [!tip]
> The originals are German rhyming verse. These are loose English versions – rewrite them freely to get the rhyme and rhythm back.

### Ballad of the Thirteen Faithful

*A drinking song, sung all over Varland.*

> Now forth they rode to face him at last,
> Lord Rabenhorst and his faithful band.
> A great sacrifice they had sworn to make,
> to wrest the realm from Rogamon's hand.
>
> Thirteen they were, all valiant souls,
> their swords and shields rang out loud.
> On the day of fate, so the story goes,
> they set out, to their cause avowed.
>
> By daring and cunning, with purpose and aim,
> they crept inside the monster's lair.
> While death raged before the city gates,
> blow followed blow in the fortress there.
>
> The king, he fought and seemed unbeatable,
> no rescue for the realm in sight.
> Left and right fell friend and comrade,
> the good lord knew: soon ends the fight.
>
> As the end drew near, blood in his ears,
> he drove his sword into the monster's breast.
> Only a few were still on their feet,
> but victory was theirs at last.
>
> The necromancer, beaten, stood before his grave,
> and there committed one last, dreadful crime:
> Lord Rabenhorst, the saviour of Varland,
> was cowardly murdered where he stood that time.
>
> Yet his sacrifice was never forgotten, in realm and in land,
> how he finally tore the kingdom from Rogamon's hand.
> So let us drink in memory of
> what the Thirteen Faithful did for us all.

> [!secret] Hidden truth
> What the ballad leaves out happened after the last blow – see [Rogamon's Reign](#rogamons-reign).

### The End of the Dragons

*A song from the mountain villages.*

> Once there were stories, yes,
> of dragons far and near,
> until one day they vanished.
>
> Not long ago at all
> they stole the cattle and more,
> until suddenly no one could find them.
>
> The dragons had gone away,
> the mountain villages sang their praise,
> but some still wondered what had happened.
>
> No more fire and no more smoke,
> and the king was very pleased,
> but some were puzzled all the same.
>
> Majestic they were, that much is true,
> as they sailed across the skies,
> but the dragons never came back.
>
> Some set out to find
> where the dragons might have gone,
> but hardly one brought word on their return.
>
> Oh, the mages pondered
> where the dragons might be,
> and whether they were gone for good.
>
> And to this day it stayed that way:
> the dragons now lived somewhere else,
> and some believed they had gone home.

### Damian the Small

*A cautionary nursery rhyme – grim, as such rhymes are.*

> Once there was Damian, who was very small.
> He had few friends, his size was his downfall.
> When he went looking for a wife, every maiden turned away.
>
> Sadly Damian took up his sword – his aim was clear.
> With quick steps he crossed the market and found his victims, all of them there.
> Without their legs, every man looks as small as Damian.
>
> The grim judge asked him the reason for this wicked game.
> Instead of building himself stilts, Damian had cut everyone else down to size.
> Damian, you mustn't do that – sad or not, you're in the wrong.
>
> Children, rest assured, Damian has been punished.
> As reward for his deed he's nothing but a head, round as a ball.
> And across the market laughter echoes: "Damian, we're taller than you again!"
