# During Rogamon's reign

<div data-embed="era" data-era="rogamons-reign"></div>

## Childhood and youth

The boy who would call himself Rogamon was taken by one of the lords of (Old) Winterglehn from a small village on the edge of the city. The lord who ordered the children of the area to be gathered was especially interested in the military potential of necromancy. Rogamon, like many others, was used for experiments to find out how necromancy – or the people with the gift – could be controlled. Little is known about the cruelties in those dungeons. What is known is that a young man broke out of one of them, killed the lord of the city with necromantic spells and fled.

He was taken in by the **Lord of Tears**, who still believed in the good use of necromancy. Rogamon showed great talent – but also a tendency towards revenge and destruction, and the two parted ways.

## The conquest of Winterglehn

A few years later the man reappeared under the name Rogamon and began to hunt and kill minor lords and nobles. Word spread that someone was avenging the injustices of past decades with necromancy, and the common people began to revolt and support him. After a small civil war he marched into Winterglehn, took the city and declared himself **king of Winterglehn and of the common people of Varland** – the official beginning of King Rogamon's reign.

The lords and the king sent armies. When the gates of Winterglehn were finally opened to them, they found that Rogamon and other necromancers had raised the fallen – and some volunteers – as undead. The armies were overwhelmed and had to flee with heavy losses. The upper classes spread the reports of undead to turn the people against Rogamon, while he extended his rule beyond the city walls.

## The siege of Norvall

One of Rogamon's greatest enemies was **King Sigmar of Norvall**, ruler of the gold fields and much of Triften and the Expansion, under whom the region had flourished. Rogamon saw himself as a liberator of the common people, not as a conqueror – which threatened everything Sigmar wanted to keep.

In his despair Sigmar turned to dark powers. Through a mysterious figure he made contact with **the Blind Seer**, a dark god banished by Aestor, who gave him power – and paranoia, cowardice and treachery.

When Rogamon's army stood before Norvall and demanded Sigmar's head in exchange for the lives of his people, Sigmar refused. When it became clear the city would fall, he ordered his soldiers to "cleanse" it: every inhabitant was dragged from their home, executed and burned, so that only ashes would be left for the Necromancer King. This night is remembered as **the Shame of Norvall**.

When his own guard, led by **Harkan Goldmund**, learned of the massacre, they turned on Sigmar and killed him – but with his last breath he cursed Harkan and his men. Rogamon found only Sigmar's dying body. In his rage he tore out Sigmar's soul and tried to destroy it forever – and felt the Blind Seer reach for him. He resisted with all his strength until a magical explosion splintered part of his soul and released many of the souls he held. Rogamon vanished. Only a red-black orb in a crater remained – and a beating heart.

## The Fields of Silence

Rogamon was believed dead, and the realm breathed again. But in the golden fields a lone figure appeared, bleeding red and black and wandering aimlessly. To keep from dying, Rogamon drew on the life and souls of countless inhabitants of the valley – and became stronger than ever. His old teacher, the Lord of Tears, confronted him there; only Rogamon walked away.

The once golden valley never recovered. The sky stayed in an eternal dusk, no sound of any living thing could be heard. **The Fields of Silence** were born.

## The conquest of the continent

Rogamon returned to Winterglehn changed – and far more radical. All resistance was wiped out and his army of undead kept growing. Triften and Silbertal were the first victims; Silbertal resisted long, but fell, and its silver was carried off to Winterglehn. **Weißküst** saved itself by swearing to cooperate and delivering everything Rogamon asked for – including people for his necromancy.

For every undead that fell, two soldiers of the kingdom fell and joined his ranks. Rogamon himself seemed invulnerable, and attempts to negotiate in the hope he would simply die of old age failed: generations later, a still youthful Rogamon entered the throne room in **Hammerfall** and declared his rule over the whole kingdom. By then many common people feared him more than they cheered him.

At the borders of Atania his armies were slowed, but not stopped. The Atanians retreated south and into hard-to-reach regions and struck back in short raids, almost bringing his campaign to a halt – but in the end a large part of Atania fell too. Rogamon returned to Winterglehn to rule his realm.

Surprisingly, he spared many institutions: the archive of Beltheim was only searched for books on necromancy, and most temples were left alone as long as the faithful did not openly oppose him. Mages, however, were hunted and killed.

## The end of Rogamon

Over time Rogamon began to see himself as a divine figure, a chosen one who had freed the land from oppression – whether because of the Blind Seer or the countless souls he carried, no one knows. Cults and churches formed in his honour, and he searched for a way to give himself divine power, while his mind slowly crumbled.

**Lord Rabenhorst** from southern Triften founded the **Paladins of the Good** and began a secret resistance, freeing occupied lands and rescuing mages and warriors. When the final battle came, a patchwork army marched on Winterglehn and fought a hopeless fight before its gates – while Lord Rabenhorst and his companions, **the 13 Faithful**, crept into the city.

They cornered Rogamon and weakened him – but even then he could not be killed. In a desperate ritual Lord Rabenhorst and the six surviving Faithful sacrificed their life force and their souls to defeat him.

> [!secret] Hidden truth
> Rogamon was never completely defeated. His body was bound to a grave, and his soul is still held by Lord Rabenhorst and the six Faithful so that it cannot return to his body and awaken again.

After Rogamon's fall the undead lost all structure. Many followed their lowest instincts and attacked the living. The end of King Rogamon is used in Varland as the beginning of a new age: **year 0 nKR**.
