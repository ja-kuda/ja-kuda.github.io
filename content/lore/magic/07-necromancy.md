# Necromancy and the Life element

The Life element encompasses all forms of interacting with souls, the dead and undead, the creation of undead and the manipulation of life force. It represents another, newer form of arcane magic – and the most feared one.

> [!glance] At a glance
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **Origin** | The death of the god Visaron, who tore the border between life and death |
> | **The Veil** | Woven by Aestor to shield the world – damaged beyond repair |
> | **First necromancer** | The Lord of Tears |
> | **Most infamous** | Rogamon, the Necromancer King |
> | **Today** | Hunted by the necromancer hunters of Varland and feared everywhere |

## Where necromancy came from

When **Visaron the Seer** was killed in the God Wars, he tore the border between life and death as he died. **Aestor** gave up his own ascension to weave **the Veil** and shield the world. But it took thousands of years until the damage showed in mortals: children were born with necromantic abilities, able to control the energies of life and death and even the undead – a dangerous counterpart to the arcane mages.

## The Lord of Tears

The first known necromancer taught others with the same gift, carefully choosing those who would use it for good: farmers learned to let the remains of their animals do certain work, mourners learned to speak to the dead to say goodbye. Every use of necromancy fed him part of the souls and life force involved, giving him an unnaturally long life.

When the lords and the crown began to feel threatened and hunted the children with the gift, one of them was Rogamon – whom the Lord of Tears took as his student. See [Legends](#legends).

## After Rogamon

After the Necromancer King's reign, the **necromancer hunters** of Varland were founded to track down and destroy dark magic; their guild has its seat in the Partition. Necromancy remains feared almost everywhere.
