# Weaving, threads and their risks

Arcane mages – **weavers** – do not cast fixed spells. They reach into the [fabric of reality](#fabric) and reshape it for their purposes. It is the freest and most flexible form of magic, and the most dangerous.

## Threads

When a weaver manipulates the fabric and restructures it, it loses structure and hold. Single **threads** come loose – exactly those threads that the weaver brings into new forms. Every spell with a real effect needs threads; only the smallest tricks – lighting a campfire, a brief small light, slightly warming a cup – get by without them.

Which threads a weaver can grasp depends on their [element](#elements): with few exceptions, a weaver controls a single element, and often only one of its forms.

## The risks

As threads are pulled out, the border between reality and the Null blurs. The more threads are pulled and the more arcane magic is worked in one place, the thinner the fabric becomes.

- **Feedback** – magic that goes wrong strikes back at the weaver, their surroundings or the spell itself.
- **Overloading** – every weaver can only pull so many threads before they need rest. Those who pull more anyway are marked.
- **Arcane scars** – the mark of overloading. They always depend on the element used and brand their bearer as a weaver for everyone to see.

> [!example]
> A lightning mage might receive a scar that twitches with electricity, a time mage a scar that looks aged, or an essence mage a scar that looks scaly or like bark.

### Rifts

In the worst case the thinned fabric **tears**, and a rift opens – often with devastating consequences. The first great rifts opened in the God Wars, between the mirror realities. Today they are rare and mostly the stuff of stories, but traces remain: the crystal fields of **the Scars** near Beltheim, said to come from a battle between two gods, and the small forest near **Feitor** on the Salt Road, known for regular rifts into the **Feywild**.

## Weavers in society

Because of these risks, weavers are watched with suspicion almost everywhere – a weaver with visible scars can expect fear rather than respect. How strongly this plays out depends on the realm; see [Magic in everyday life](#everyday-magic).
