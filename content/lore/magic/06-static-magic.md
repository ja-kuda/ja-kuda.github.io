# Static magic

Static magic is the scholarly answer to the [risks of weaving](#weaving). It is the more scientific approach to manipulating the fabric of existence: its spells were "encapsulated" – their effects were isolated over time and refined so far that they still need threads, but no longer tear the fabric. The threads are only **borrowed**; after the effect has run its course, they weave back into their natural structure.

A static mage doesn't have to fear that a spell fails, turns against them or endangers their surroundings. In exchange they are bound to what their spells can do: static spells have precisely defined functions and reproducible effects – no improvising, no surprises.

Static spells are still magic, though, and require complex formulas and rituals. Static mages spend a large part of their lives finding and learning spells, and tend to carry collections of formulas and rituals.

Because it is safe and can be taught, static magic is often the most accepted form of magic – see [Magic in everyday life](#everyday-magic).
